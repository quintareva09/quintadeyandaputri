# Making limited publishing count: an ICI-aligned content system for KBRI Wina

## 1. The challenge

KBRI produces content every week: visits, ceremonies, cultural events, consular information, bilateral meetings, community events. All of it is legitimate, and all of it follows mission activity.

The operating environment has changed:
- **Fewer slots.** With a small communications team and fewer publishing opportunities than in previous years, each post now carries more weight.
- **A strategic question from leadership.** The DCM has asked how to make Austrians see Indonesia as more than culture and tourism.

What the current, informal model does not answer is this: **over a month or a year, what image of Indonesia are these posts building, taken together?** No system decides how the limited publishing opportunities contribute to Indonesia's image in Austria.

> **The challenge:** how can KBRI turn a limited number of social-media posts into a deliberate, measurable effort to shape Indonesia's image, across the dimensions the Ministry already measures?

Put simply: the question is not *"what should we post?"* It is *"with limited opportunities to post, what do we want Austrians to understand about Indonesia over time?"*

This does not say KBRI has been communicating wrongly. KBRI already has formats for every dimension of Indonesia's image. What is missing is a rule for how much of each: an informal model was enough when there were more posts and no explicit image priority, and it is no longer enough.

**Scope.** Instagram (@inainvienna) is where this was measured. The same allocation logic applies to every KBRI channel.

### In brief
- **What the account shows today** (provisional, §3). The allocation is not governed by any image priority:
  - About **three quarters of posts (720 of 992)** serve none of the ICI's three dimensions. These are mostly service, protocol and ceremonial content.
  - Of the posts that do, about **72% are Culture & Tourism**.
  - **Governance & Safety has had no posts since January 2025** (0 of 117).
- **Proposal** (§4): separate the account's two jobs:
  - an **image stream** allocated across the three ICI dimensions, with one caption standard, one visual specification and one language rule;
  - a **service stream** for citizens and protocol, which protects that mandated work.
- **Measurement** (§5): track allocation and audience reach, not likes.
- **First month** (§6): validate the coding, pull audience data from Instagram Insights, and agree the targets.

## 2. Why the ICI, and what it gives KBRI

The Ministry already measures Indonesia's image abroad with the **Indonesia Image Index (ICI)**, on three dimensions:
- **A** Culture & Tourism
- **B** Economy & Business
- **C** Governance & Safety

Aligning content planning to it gives KBRI five things:

- **Communications becomes an allocation problem that can be managed.** With fewer posts, each slot is a larger share of the mission's external communication, so choosing what gets published matters more.
- **The DCM's concern becomes operational.** Instead of "we should show more economy and governance", KBRI can track how much attention goes to each of A, B, C and service content, month by month.
- **Pensosbud gains a management KPI.** The unit can report not only output and engagement but which image dimensions were communicated. Once Instagram Insights is in place (§6), it can also report to which audience.
- **The Ministry's instrument becomes usable at mission level.** No new framework is invented, and results can be read in the Ministry's own terms.
- **The planning logic survives staff rotation.** Priorities are written down in a codebook and a target allocation rather than held in individual memory.

## 4. The proposal

### 4.1 Two streams, one account
| Stream | Purpose | Content | Audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Image stream** | Build perception on the three ICI dimensions | Pillars A, B, C (§4.2) | Austrian public |
| **Service stream** | Inform and serve | Consular, protocol, community, greetings, multilateral (codebook D1–D5) | Indonesian citizens; partners |

- *Basis:* F1. Separating the streams protects the service content, which is mandated, while making the image stream's allocation visible and plannable.
- *Format of the service stream* — candidates: Instagram Stories with saved Highlights, a reduced share of grid posts, or both. **[judgement call]** Any change of format should be agreed with the account owner, who knows the citizen audience's habits.

### 4.2 Three pillars
| Pillar | ICI attributes | Example formats (already used by the account, unless noted) | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A: Indonesia to visit and experience** | A1 visit, A2 culture, A3 plural & tolerant | Destination features, cultural events in Austria, BIPA classes. **New:** pluralism stories (A3 has 4 posts in 5 years) **[judgement call]** | F2: already strong; keep, don't grow |
| **B: Indonesia to do business with** | B1 stable, B2 business, B3 products | Business notices for Austrian firms, trade events, product showcases (coffee, crafts). **New:** a recurring "Indonesia in numbers" explainer **[judgement call]** | F2: 50 posts in 5 years |
| **C: Indonesia that works** | C1 safe, C2 democratic, C3 clear regulations | Visa and entry explainers, which are the account's most-used C format (16 posts). **New:** "how it works" explainers on elections, institutions and investment licensing, written for foreigners **[judgement call]** | F3: no posts since Jan 2025; the DCM names this dimension as the weak side |

- **Conversion rule** (codebook Rule 6). Many service items have a foreign-audience version. Examples:
  - A visa-regulation notice → a visa explainer for Austrians (C3).
  - A multilateral statement with domestic content → C. An example is Indonesia's national nuclear-safety regime presented at the IAEA.

  This raises B/C output without new sources. **[judgement call on feasibility]**
- **Syndication.** About a third of past B and C posts were reposts. Reposts from Ministry accounts can carry the pillars, but they should be **framed for the Austrian reader** in German or English. **[judgement call]**

### 4.3 Allocation target for the image stream
- **Direction (supported by F2, F3 and the DCM's assessment):** raise the shares of B and C relative to A.
- **Starting numbers:** **[judgement call]**. For example, about 40% A / 30% B / 30% C of image-stream posts, reviewed after two quarters.
  - Nothing in the data shows which split changes perception. These numbers are a planning device, not a finding.
  - They should be revised once perception data from a non-event sample exists (§5).

### 4.4 Caption standard
- **Every image-stream post carries at least one concrete fact about Indonesia** for its pillar: a figure, a place, a procedure, a policy, a product. Naming a topic is not enough ("discussed economic cooperation" does not qualify). *(Basis: codebook Rule 3; F1)*
- **The pillar is tagged before publication**, using the codebook's labels.

### 4.5 Language
- **Image stream:** German first, with English as a second language. **[judgement call]** *(Basis: F4. The audience's actual language mix is unknown until Insights data is in)*
- **Service stream:** Indonesian, as now.

### 4.6 Visual specification
- **Specify a palette and templates; there is nothing existing to codify.** *(Basis: F5)*
- **Contents:** **[judgement call]**
  - one primary accent colour, with one variant per pillar so the allocation shows on the grid;
  - one type family;
  - a template for explainers (B and C) and one for event photography (A and service).
- **Not covered by the evidence.** Typography, logo placement and photo style were not measured, so these choices rest on design judgement only.

### 4.7 Cadence
- **There is no current cadence target.** Publishing volume has varied widely: about 360 posts in 2022 (excluding the daily COVID series), 69 in 2025. *(Basis: posts-per-year counts)*
- **Proposed rhythm:** a fixed weekly rhythm, for example 2–3 image-stream posts per week rotating A / B / C, with service content as needed. **[judgement call]** The data says nothing about the best frequency.

## 5. How success is measured
| Measure | What it tells us | Frequency | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Allocation** — share of image-stream posts per pillar vs target | Whether the plan is being followed | Monthly; `analysis.R` on a fresh scrape | F1–F3; the instrument used here |
| **Coding reliability** — Cohen's kappa between two human coders, or human vs automated | Whether the allocation figures can be trusted | Once at start, then quarterly (30 posts) | Method |
| **Audience reach** — share of followers and reach located in Austria (Instagram Insights) | Whether the intended audience is reached at all | Monthly | Not available today |
| **Perception** — ICI items asked of a sample **not** recruited at Indonesian events | Whether perception differs across the dimensions | Yearly, or per campaign | Event samples are self-selected and favour A; indicative only |
| **Visual compliance** — share of posts within the specified palette | Whether the specification is applied | Quarterly; colour step of `analysis.R` | F5; baseline 71% neutral, no accent colour |
| **Engagement** — likes, comments, saves | Context only | Monthly | **Not a success criterion.** Observational engagement differences between content types are confounded and do not show what works |

**What this measurement can and cannot claim:**
- It can show whether the allocation changed and whether Austrians are reached.
- It **cannot** show that the account changed perceptions of Indonesia. That would need a design the account does not have, such as comparing exposed and unexposed audiences. No claim of impact should be made from these measures.

## 6. Implementation
| When | Action | Who **[judgement call]** |
|---|---|---|
| Month 1 | Hand-code 50 posts (30 random + 20 B/C) and compute kappa. If it is below 0.6, revise the codebook and re-run. | Account owner + Pensosbud intern |
| Month 1 | Export Instagram Insights: audience country, reach per post | Account owner |
| Month 1 | Agree the pillar targets (§4.3) and the service-stream format (§4.1) | Head of Pensosbud, with DCM sign-off |
| Month 1–2 | Visual specification and 2–3 templates | Pensosbud |
| Months 2–4 | Pilot: tag every post A / B / C / service; monthly allocation report | Account owner |
| Month 6 | Review allocation, reach and coding reliability; adjust targets | Head of Pensosbud |

- **The account owner leads.** They hold the institutional knowledge of the citizen audience, the approval chain and what is feasible.
- **The codebook and script are tools for them,** not an external audit.

## 7. Limits of this proposal
- **The content figures are unvalidated** (see the status box). The direction of F1–F3 holds across every coding setting tested, but the exact percentages may move after validation.
- **Nothing here shows** that changing the allocation will change how Austrians perceive Indonesia. The case rests on aligning the account to the Ministry's own instrument and the DCM's stated priority, not on demonstrated effect.
- **The audience is unknown.** If Insights shows that few followers are in Austria, reach becomes the first problem, before allocation.
- **Coverage is incomplete.** The scrape covers the 1,000 most recent grid posts only; no Stories, no history before December 2021.
